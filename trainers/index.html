What is a Store Pack?

A Store Pack is a small training file for one location. It can contain your store name, PLU codes, item names, categories, sizes, custom study sets, and photos.

When a cashier imports it, PLUed builds a My Store track around the items you chose. The national produce library stays intact. Your pack adds the local layer: the apple sizes you stock, the seasonal table, or a house code that only your register team uses.

Good pack, not giant pack: include what a cashier can actually ring at your location. A clean 120-item pack beats a 600-row dump nobody trusts.

Build one in 10 minutes

  1. Open the Pack Builder. Enter the store name. The draft stays in that browser while you work.
  2. Add codes. Enter the PLU, item name, category, and optional size. Add a photo from your device when your store's item needs a specific visual.
  3. Create sets. Group items into useful drills such as Front Table, Organic Wall, Seasonal, or New Hire Week One.
  4. Fix any validation notes. The share tools unlock when required fields are clean and codes are usable.
  5. Download the .plupack. Keep it as your editable, offline backup.
  6. Turn on hosted sharing if you want one QR with photos. Print the QR and place it where new hires train.

Already have a pack? Use Open an existing .plupack to edit. The builder restores its codes, sets, photos, and pack identity.

Bulk import from a spreadsheet

For a longer wall, do not type every row. Choose Import CSV / ZIP in the builder.

ColumnRequired?What goes here
codeYesThe PLU or store code. Keep leading zeroes when your system uses them.
nameYesThe cashier-facing item name.
categoryYesA supported produce category such as fruit or veg.
sizeNoLarge, small, bunch, each, or another useful register distinction.
photoNoThe matching image filename, such as bananas.jpg.

CSV only

Import a CSV when you only need codes and names. The builder accepts common header names and reports rows it cannot use.

ZIP with images

Put one CSV and the referenced image files into a ZIP. The images can be in folders; the builder matches them by filename, downscales them for the pack, and warns you about missing photos without throwing away the row.

Hosted QR and link

Delivers codes and photos. Put one poster in the breakroom and let cashiers scan from PLUed.

.plupack file

Carries codes, sets, and photos in one offline backup. Keep this file so you can edit later.

A hosted pack keeps the same pack ID when you open the existing .plupack and upload it again. That means the same QR can deliver the updated pack. Cashiers who already imported an older version should scan or import again to pull the update.

Do not rely on a code-only offline link for photos. Hosted links and .plupack files carry photos. Offline links do not.

The share checkbox and what gets sent

The builder includes an unchecked box labeled Share this pack with the PLUed team to help improve the app. Nothing is sent unless you turn it on.

When checked, the builder sends the store name, codes, and photos in the pack to PLUed's hosted pack service. That upload creates the short hosted link that lets a QR deliver photos. Anyone with that link can open the pack, so treat the QR like a handout, not a secret.

If you do not check the box, you can still download the complete .plupack file. The fallback share link is code-only.

Need a hosted pack removed? Email support@plued.app with the store name.

Trainer FAQ

Can I edit a pack later?

Yes. Keep the downloaded .plupack, then choose Open an existing .plupack to edit in the builder. Download a fresh backup after every change.

Do new hires need to pay?

The app is free to start. Most Common Chapter 1 is free. The full training journey and full My Store training unlock with a one-time in-app purchase, not a subscription.

Does the QR include photos?

Hosted QR codes do. A QR using the older offline link format contains codes only. The builder tells you which type it produced.

Can I use our own item photos?

Yes. Add them row by row or include them in a ZIP with your spreadsheet. Use clear, close photos that look like what reaches your checkout.
