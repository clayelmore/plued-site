What is a Store Pack?

A Store Pack is a small training file for one location. It can contain your store name, PLU codes, item names, categories, sizes, custom study sets, and a PLUed catalog graphic for every item.

When a cashier imports it, PLUed builds a My Store track around the items you chose. The national produce library stays intact. Your pack adds the local layer: the apple sizes you stock, the seasonal table, or a house code that only your register team uses.

Sets are your drills. Each set you create in the builder (Front Table, Organic Wall, New Hire Week One) arrives in the cashier's app as a named practice collection under your store's name. A cashier can study, practice, or test exactly that group of items, in the order your floor actually works. A pack with three good sets is a training plan, not just a code list.

Good pack, not giant pack: include what a cashier can actually ring at your location. A clean 120-item pack beats a 600-row dump nobody trusts.

Build one in 10 minutes

  1. Open the Pack Builder. Enter the store name. The draft stays in that browser while you work.
  2. Add codes. Enter the PLU, item name, category, and optional size. For the picture, tap Use PLUed image and pick from PLUed's own produce art. Your "Sweet Vintage Orange" can wear the orange it actually looks like. Truly novel item? Pick the closest match as a stand-in and submit the pack to the PLUed team, and we build the real graphic.
  3. Create sets. Group items into useful drills such as Front Table, Organic Wall, Seasonal, or New Hire Week One. Each one becomes a named practice collection in the cashier's app.
  4. Fix any validation notes. The share tools unlock when required fields are clean and codes are usable.
  5. Download the .plupack. Keep it as your editable, offline backup.
  6. Copy the share link or print the QR. The link is instant and self-contained: codes, sets, and graphics all travel inside it, and it works even offline. A copy of the pack is also sent to PLUed for content screening. Place the QR where new hires train.

Already have a pack? Paste your share link into "Open your published pack": the builder restores its codes, sets, graphics, and identity from the link, so re-sharing keeps the same QR working. (A downloaded .plupack file still works too, as an offline backup.)

Bulk import from a spreadsheet

For a longer wall, do not type every row. Choose Import CSV / XLSX in the builder — a plain .csv file or an Excel .xlsx workbook both import directly, so most register exports work as-is.

ColumnRequired?What goes here
codeYesThe PLU or store code. Keep leading zeroes when your system uses them.
nameYesThe cashier-facing item name.
categoryYesA supported produce category such as fruit or veg.
sizeNoXS, S, M, L, or XL. Register words work too — "Large" maps to L, and "Each" or "All Sizes" mean no size. Leave blank when size does not matter.
descriptionNoWhat the item looks like ("large green apple, waxy skin"). Used with the name to auto-match a PLUed graphic.

Graphics match themselves

On import, the builder reads each row's name and description and picks the best-fit PLUed image automatically. The rows become your review table. Keep a match, or tap any graphic to swap it using the same picker as a typed row. The builder accepts common header names (a register export whose name column is called "Description" still works) and reports rows it cannot use.

Graphics tip: most "custom" codes are ordinary produce. A store-coded sweet potato is still a sweet potato. Pick its PLUed catalog image and the graphic travels inside the plain share link and QR, and it looks like the rest of the app. A close match is fine. It stands in until PLUed adds the exact item.

Publishing, submissions, and what gets sent

Your link works on its own. PLUed catalog images ride inside the share link itself, so a pack shares as a self-contained link that opens even offline. When you share, a copy of the pack also goes to PLUed for content screening; no account is needed. Anyone with the link can open it, so treat the QR like a handout, not a secret.

Submissions (always your choice). The builder has an unchecked box labeled Submit this pack to the PLUed team to consider for the app. Tick it if you'd like your store's codes considered for the built-in catalog, or to request a real graphic for a novel item (describe it in the note). An optional "who is this from?" line lets us follow up. Nothing is flagged for review unless you tick the box.

Acceptable use. Keep pack content lawful and workplace-appropriate: item names, notes, and any hosted content are subject to review, and packs that break this rule are removed without notice. Hosted packs are unlisted (reachable only by their exact link).

Need a hosted pack removed, yours or one you were sent? Email support@plued.app with the store name or pack link.

Trainer FAQ

Can I edit a pack later?

Yes, paste your share link into Open your published pack in the builder and keep editing. No file to keep track of. (A downloaded .plupack also works, as an offline backup.)

Do new hires need to pay?

The app is free to start. Level 1 (The Daily Dozen) is free. The full journey, every Store Section and Recipe Set, and full My Store training unlock with a one-time in-app purchase, not a subscription.

Does the QR include the graphics?

Yes. PLUed catalog images travel inside the link and QR themselves, and the link works offline forever. Sharing also sends a screened copy of the pack to PLUed.

What about items PLUed has no image for?

Pick the closest match. It stands in until PLUed adds the exact item. Then submit the pack with a short description of what the item looks like, and if it belongs in the catalog we build the real graphic; every store gets it in the next update.

Does PLUed work for Spanish-speaking crews?

Yes. The whole app is available in Spanish (beta): menus, all 1,087 produce names, concepts, and coaching notes. Cashiers switch it themselves from Settings → Language; your Store Pack's codes and graphics work the same in either language.

Training more than one store?

Store plans put every cashier on the same page.

Seats

A store plan buys seats for a whole location. The store gets one word for its cashiers, its own codes and graphics in the app, and its name on its own training track. Nobody at the store creates an account, and nothing is installed on store computers. Chains and co-ops can set one plan up for every location at once.
